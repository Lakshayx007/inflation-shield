Frequently Asked Questions

What this dashboard shows, what it doesn't, and how to use it.

Inflation Shield is an early-warning system for Indian large-cap companies. For each firm, it produces a single 0–100 score called MSSMargin Squeeze Score: a 0–100 ranking of how vulnerable a firm is to margin compression. (Margin Squeeze Score) that estimates the risk of EBITDAEarnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization. A measure of operating profitability. margin compression in the next two quarters. A high MSS means the firm is more likely to see its profitability squeezed by input-cost inflation.
Think of MSSMargin Squeeze Score: a 0–100 ranking of how vulnerable a firm is to margin compression. as a relative risk ranking. A firm at MSS 80 is materially more vulnerable than one at MSS 30. Specific bands: below 40 is low risk, 40–70 is moderate, 70 and above is high. The score is not a probability — it shouldn’t be read as “an 80% chance of compression.” It’s an ordinal ranking, like a credit rating.
On the 2022–2023 test set, the model achieved an AUCArea Under the Curve: 0.5 is random, 1.0 is perfect. Measures how well the model separates risky firms from safe ones. of 0.73 (real-world range 0.67–0.73 once you account for cross-validation). For comparison, a coin flip is 0.50; 1.00 would be perfect prediction. The model correctly flagged 38% of true compression events two quarters ahead — a meaningful lift over the naive sector-baseline of around 23%, but not a guarantee. Treat MSS as one input among many, not a final answer.
We deliberately backtested on the 2022–2023 inflation cycle — a real historical period when WPI hit 10%, Brent crude crossed $115, and the rupee fell from 54 to 68 against the dollar. Backtesting on a known-outcome window is how this kind of model gets validated. The same model architecture, retrained on 2022–2025 data, would produce live forward-looking scores. The architecture is the deliverable; current-quarter retraining is operational work.
Six engineered indicators capture different dimensions of vulnerability: CPRCost Pass-through Ratio: when input costs rise, how much can the firm raise output prices? (Cost Pass-through Ratio — can the firm raise prices when costs rise?), ICSIInput Cost Sensitivity Index: how exposed the firm is to volatile raw material prices. (Input Cost Sensitivity — how exposed is it to volatile raw materials?), IBDInventory Buffer Days: how many days of raw material the firm holds in stock as protection against price spikes. (Inventory Buffer Days — does it have a stockpile to ride out shocks?), WCSSWorking Capital Stress Score: net difference between days the firm waits to collect from customers vs. days it takes to pay suppliers. (Working Capital Stress), OLOperating Leverage: share of fixed costs in the firm's cost structure. Higher = margins more volatile when sales swing. (Operating Leverage), and SIBSector Inflation Beta: how much the firm's margins move when its sector's average margin moves. Like a stock beta but for margins. (Sector Inflation Beta). The model also uses lagged margin trends and a sector control. Click any firm to see which factors are pushing its score up or down.
No. This is academic research, not financial advice. MSS surfaces firms for deeper analysis — it does not replace fundamental analysis, valuation work, or qualitative judgment. A high MSS means “investigate further,” not “sell.” A low MSS means “no immediate concern from this lens,” not “safe to buy.”
Our universe is 30 large-cap NSE/BSE firms. Many sectors in this sample are represented by a single firm (e.g., RIL is the only refinery, BHEL the only boilers & turbines firm). We show sector-level scores for completeness, but for single-firm sectors the “sector score” is really just the firm’s score. A production version would expand the universe to several hundred firms for meaningful sector aggregation.
Three honest limitations. First, working-capital data (inventory, trade receivables/payables) is missing for 70%+ of firm-quarters in our source data, so two of the six features (IBDInventory Buffer Days: how many days of raw material the firm holds in stock as protection against price spikes., WCSSWorking Capital Stress Score: net difference between days the firm waits to collect from customers vs. days it takes to pay suppliers.) are sparse. Second, we have no macro overlay — no oil price, no WPI, no INR/USD as direct inputs. Third, we end in 2024, so the model has not seen current conditions. Each of these is a future enhancement.

    Model

    Understanding how the model works and its limitations.

    How reliable is this model?

    On historical out-of-sample data (2022-2023), the model correctly separated firms heading toward margin compression from those that weren’t with 73% accuracy (technically: AUCArea Under the Curve: 0.5 is random, 1.0 is perfect. Measures how well the model separates risky firms from safe ones. 0.73). The model is stable — across five different randomized retraining runs, the score stays within ±0.6 percentage points. We chose this simpler model over a more complex alternative (XGBoost) because it’s more reproducible and the performance is statistically equivalent.

    What this means in practice: trust the relative ranking of firms more than the absolute MSS number. A firm at MSS 80 is meaningfully more at risk than one at MSS 30. The exact numeric difference matters less than the rank order.

    What this model can’t do

    This model is trained on 2022-2023 data and has not seen the 2020 pandemic, the 2022 commodity spike, or current market conditions. It does not predict crashes, default risk, or stock returns — only operating margin compression, two quarters ahead. It works best when combined with qualitative judgment, not as a sole input to investment decisions.

    To deploy this in production, we would need to retrain on 2022-2025 quarterly data and add macroeconomic features (oil prices, INR/USD, Indian WPI inflation). That’s the natural next step.

    Methodology

    How a firm's MSS is calculated: six engineered features plus lag/momentum inputs feed a tuned LightGBM model.

    Data: CMIE Prowess quarterly financials, 30 large NSE/BSE-listed Indian firms, 2005-2024. Test set = 2022-2023.

    Target: Binary indicator of at least 150 bps EBITDA margin compression over next 2 quarters.

    Model: Tuned LightGBM (Optuna, 50 trials), 23 features including CPR, ICSI, IBD, WCSS, OL, SIB plus lag and momentum features.

    Limitations: 30-firm universe, ends 2024, inventory/working-capital fields sparse (IBDInventory Buffer Days: how many days of raw material the firm holds in stock as protection against price spikes. and WCSSWorking Capital Stress Score: net difference between days the firm waits to collect from customers vs. days it takes to pay suppliers. computable for less than 30% of rows), no macro features (WPI/Brent/INR-USD).
